01 — About

What I work on

I lead thin-film photovoltaics R&D at imec / EnergyVille, where my work sits in the gap between a champion perovskite cell on a square centimetre and a module that holds its efficiency in the field. Most of what limits perovskite commercialisation appears only at scale, so as technical and scientific lead I drive a module-scaling methodology that connects nanoscale material properties to large-area device performance — validated from roughly 1 cm² to 900 cm².

The second half of that argument is evidence. I led the first multi-site outdoor monitoring study of perovskite modules, running over two years across three countries, and used it to build machine-learning lifetime forecasts. Bankability is a data problem before it is a technology problem, and utilities and insurers need degradation numbers with the measurement conditions attached.

I trained as an engineer at Delhi College of Engineering, University of Delhi (B.Eng. in Polymer Science & Chemical Technology, first class), completing my bachelor’s thesis research on conducting-polymer nanostructures during an internship at the University of Auckland, New Zealand. I went on to a Ph.D. at Nanyang Technological University in Singapore on molecular hole-transport materials, then started a perovskite programme from scratch as a postdoctoral fellow at CNRS in France. A Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellowship at EPFL (STARS, 2019–2021) with Prof. Michael Grätzel produced the nanoscale interface engineering that underpins the scaling work. I have been at imec since 2022, where my work spans the research and its valorisation toward industrial deployment.

02 — Research

From molecule to field

Materials

The stack, layer by layer

Light absorbers across bandgaps — from 2D/3D mixed-dimensional to wide-gap compositions — with molecular passivators, charge-transport layers, self-assembled monolayers, and electrodes designed as a system.

Interfaces

Stability by design

Nanoscale interface and defect engineering that decouples the mechanisms driving light-, heat- and bias-induced degradation in metal-halide perovskites.

Scale-up

From cells to modules

A module-scaling methodology linking nanoscale film formation to large-area performance, validated across nearly three orders of magnitude in aperture area.

Reliability

Evidence from outdoors

Multi-site outdoor monitoring over two years and three countries, with machine-learning lifetime forecasting — the degradation data the industry needs for bankability.
